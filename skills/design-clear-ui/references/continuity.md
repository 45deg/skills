# Work and Recovery

Apply to inputs, decisions, or processing that are costly to reconstruct. Disposable interactions may need only direct revision.

## Define what must survive

Define the required return boundary before implementation, including reload for substantial web work unless explicit constraints require otherwise. Do not narrow it afterward to match what was implemented. Do not assume cross-device retention. Explaining a loss does not satisfy required preservation.

Preserve what continuation needs: raw drafts and targets, manual changes, selections, pending request identity, job settings, resources, or results as applicable. Do not introduce these categories when the task has no use for them. Explain the actual retention scope and deliberate clearing.

Verify preserved work and understandable continuation separately using [verification.md](verification.md).

## Preserve identity and independence

Reordering, regeneration, or a missing option must not redirect a pending operation. If a target becomes unavailable, retain its identity and entered values; offer reconciliation that resolves the actual conflict or deliberate disposal. Verify restoration collisions as well as missing objects.

Changing, recovering, or discarding one unit must preserve unrelated work. Pending-work recovery cannot depend solely on a global undo slot that later actions overwrite. Closing is not discarding, and discarded work must not reappear on return.

## Match recovery to the effect

For costly reversible replacement or deletion, retain a coherent before-state including manual changes. Keep promised recovery across the same boundary as the work. Choose a recovery scope appropriate to the work and explain its expiry before an operation consumes it. Confirmation is not restoration.

For accepted or irreversible effects, preserve request identity and resolve authoritative status before retry. A lost response is not rejection; local clearing does not reverse acceptance. Return must not replay effects unless explicitly required and safe.

For asynchronous work, keep per-job settings distinct from later defaults. Pause, cancel, retry, and discard must affect their stated scope. Represent limits on stopping in-flight work honestly. Retrying one failure must not repeat successes.

If resources need reattachment, preserve decisions and verify the intended identity; filenames alone are insufficient. Explain the remaining user action rather than claim the work is fully restored.

Avoid unnecessary time limits. Where expiry is required, show its effect before users commit time, offer extension when permitted, and preserve work through reauthentication where safe. Do not rely only on a brief warning that an absent user may miss.

## Handle storage failure

Claim saved only after a successful write. Keep live work and offer a visible intentional retry or usable fallback covering the affected working set. A result export may omit unfinished decisions. Protect unreadable saved content rather than replace it with empty defaults. Exercise recovery through completion, not just the error message.
